The Art of Readable Web Typography

By Ava Stone · 6 min read

Good typography is invisible. Readers should never think about the font, the spacing or the width of a line; they should simply read. CSS gives you every tool you need, from font-size to letter-spacing, and learning to combine them is a superpower.

Start with a comfortable base size and scale everything with rem units so your page respects what the user has chosen in their browser settings. A line-height around 1.6 gives paragraphs room to breathe.

Typography is what language looks like. Treat it with the same care as the words themselves.

Three quick rules

  • Keep lines between 50 and 75 characters.
  • Use no more than two font families.
  • Create hierarchy with size, weight and colour.

Finally, remember details like text-transform, hyphens and ::first-letter. They are small touches that make an article feel designed rather than merely styled.
